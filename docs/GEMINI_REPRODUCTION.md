## 1. Data source and hash

- **Source:** NASA POWER (Prediction Of Worldwide Energy Resources) Monthly & Annual API, NASA Langley Research Center; meteorological parameters from MERRA-2 (NASA GMAO)
- **Request:** `https://power.larc.nasa.gov/api/temporal/monthly/point?parameters=T2M,PRECTOTCORR,RH2M&community=AG&longitude=76.97&latitude=52.29&start=2001&end=2024&format=JSON`
- **Retrieved:** 2026-11-14T04:58:11.069Z
- **How:** HTTPS GET issued from a Chrome browser on the team's computer (the build sandbox had no route to power.larc.nasa.gov); SHA-256 computed in the browser on the response body and matched after the body was stored in the repository (byte-identical)
- **Product:** POWER Monthly and Annual API v2.10.0; sources MERRA2, POWER; time standard LST
- **Kind of data:** model reanalysis (MERRA-2), not a station or a direct satellite measurement
- **Resolution:** MERRA-2 native grid, 0.5 deg latitude x 0.625 deg longitude; a point query returns the enclosing grid cell, at the grid cell's mean elevation (per POWER documentation); grid-cell mean elevation 110.97 m
- **Coverage:** 2001-01 to 2024-12, 288 months, 0 missing
- **SHA-256 (raw bytes, 14282 B):** `8a65f9e56b005f77eec3783a09903ad2842031590cc0c8084441d92b19f87de6`
- **SHA-256 (data only, ignoring server timings):** `534e047eb571b1e1ddb2e7681ca4564e90d43d2faa0779ea46498a9578757462`
- **Raw file:** `data/raw/power/power_monthly_point_lat52.29_lon76.97_2001-2024_PRECTOTCORR-RH2M-T2M_AG.json` · provenance record in `data/provenance/`

Whether Gemini used exactly the same response cannot be checked: it gave no request or hash. Agreement to three decimals on 16 numbers makes a different dataset very unlikely.

## 2. Data traps (brief §1.1)

| Gemini's warning | Found in the real response | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `YYYY13` keys are an annual summary, not a 13th month | 24 such keys per variable (312 keys = 24 × 13). They equal the day-weighted mean of the 12 months to within 0.0050 (two-decimal rounding); a plain 12-month mean is off by up to 0.16 for RH2M | **Confirmed** |
| Missing values may be `-999.0` | The header declares `fill_value: -999.0`. Occurrences in this file: 0 | **Confirmed as declared**; not exercised by this file |
| `PRECTOTCORR` may be in mm/day | Response metadata: `mm/day` | **Confirmed** |

Consequence of the unit: mean annual precipitation is 325 mm when each month is multiplied by its real length; multiplying by 30 misstates single years by up to 10.2 mm.

Two traps Gemini did not mention:

1. **The raw hash is not stable.** Each response contains a `times` block with server timings, so two downloads of identical data have different SHA-256 values. We record both the raw hash and a hash of the data alone.
2. **A point is a grid cell.** The third coordinate in the response is the cell's mean elevation. For Pavlodar it is 111 m (plausible). For Almaty it is 2478 m, although the city lies near 800 m: the cell averages mountains and plain, and its temperatures are not the city's.

## 4. Trends (brief §1.3)

**Explanation.** Gemini evidently applied the Theil–Sen slope and the classic Mann–Kendall test to the 24 annual values that the API itself supplies under the `YYYY13` keys. Those values are rounded to two decimals, which creates ties and shifts the p-values slightly compared with annual means computed from the months (variant B: 0.413, 0.264, 0.035). That is why our own estimates differ from Gemini's in the second or third decimal of p while the conclusions are identical. This is an inference from the match; Gemini did not confirm its method.

**Verdict: REPRODUCED** (slopes, p-values and the FDR value).

### What a more careful test says

Our primary analysis uses annual means computed from the monthly values, the Theil–Sen slope with its 95% interval, and the larger p of two autocorrelation-aware tests (Hamed–Rao modified Mann–Kendall; least squares with AR(1) effective sample size).

| Variable | Slope (95% CI) | Classic MK p | Hamed–Rao p | LSQ AR(1) p | Primary p | q | Detectable at 80% power |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| T2M | -0.0212 (-0.0666 to +0.0464) °C/yr | 0.413 | 0.413 | 0.578 | 0.578 | 0.578 | ≥ 0.095 °C/yr |
| PRECTOTCORR | +0.0048 (-0.0041 to +0.0152) mm/day/yr | 0.264 | 0.264 | 0.147 | 0.264 | 0.397 | ≥ 0.014 mm/day/yr |
| RH2M | +0.1645 (+0.0072 to +0.2934) pp/yr | 0.035 | 0.035 | 0.010 | 0.035 | 0.105 | ≥ 0.190 pp/yr |

**Temperature.** The statement “the trend is not significant” is reproduced. The statement “there is no warming” would not be: the 95% interval runs from -0.67 to +0.46 °C per decade, and a record this short and this variable could only be expected to reveal trends above about 0.9 °C per decade. Warming or cooling of several tenths of a degree per decade is fully compatible with these data.

**Relative humidity.** The only nominally significant trend (p = 0.035) is fragile:

- after correcting for three tests, q = 0.105 (Gemini reported the same);
- leaving out any one of the years 2002, 2008, 2017, 2018, 2024 moves p across 0.05 (range 0.015–0.102); 2024 is the most humid year of the record;
- in 1981–2000 the slope has the opposite sign (-0.192 pp/yr), and over 1981–2024 it is +0.027 pp/yr with p = 0.436.

So the humidity increase is a feature of the 2001–2024 window, not an established long-term trend.

## 5. Lag analysis (brief §1.4)

Definition used here: r(k) = corr(T[t], RH[t+k]) on monthly anomalies. **k = +1 pairs temperature with the following month's humidity**; k = −1 pairs temperature with the previous month's humidity.

| +5 |  | -0.1011 |  | -0.0922 | 0.345 |
| +6 |  | +0.0357 |  | +0.0511 | 0.501 |

- **Lag 0 and lag +1: REPRODUCED** (−0.316 and −0.185). Gemini's “lag +1” is temperature leading humidity; the opposite direction is much weaker (k = −1: -0.081).
- **“About −0.04 at 2+ months”: PARTLY.** It holds at k = +2 (-0.045) but not as a rule: k = +3 gives -0.090, k = +6 gives +0.036.
- **Multiple testing.** Thirteen lags were scanned. After Benjamini–Hochberg correction with autocorrelation-adjusted p-values, the lags that remain significant for T–RH are: +0, +1.
- **A counterexample to “no memory”.** For precipitation and humidity the association persists: k = +1: +0.33, k = +2: +0.16, k = +3: +0.13, k = +4: +0.19; significant after correction at k = +1, +2, +4.

**What may be said:** in this grid cell, temperature anomalies and the following month's humidity anomalies are weakly negatively correlated. **What may not:** that temperature drives next month's humidity, or that the atmosphere has (or lacks) long-term memory. A lag is a timing statistic; monthly means cannot resolve sub-monthly processes; persistence of either series alone spreads a same-month association onto neighbouring lags.
