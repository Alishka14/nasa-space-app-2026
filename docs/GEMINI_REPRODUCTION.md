## 2. Data traps (brief §1.1)

| Gemini's warning | Found in the real response | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| `YYYY13` keys are an annual summary, not a 13th month | 24 such keys per variable (312 keys = 24 × 13). They equal the day-weighted mean of the 12 months to within 0.0050 (two-decimal rounding); a plain 12-month mean is off by up to 0.16 for RH2M | **Confirmed** |
| Missing values may be `-999.0` | The header declares `fill_value: -999.0`. Occurrences in this file: 0 | **Confirmed as declared**; not exercised by this file |
| `PRECTOTCORR` may be in mm/day | Response metadata: `mm/day` | **Confirmed** |

Consequence of the unit: mean annual precipitation is 325 mm when each month is multiplied by its real length; multiplying by 30 misstates single years by up to 10.2 mm.

Two traps Gemini did not mention:

1. **The raw hash is not stable.** Each response contains a `times` block with server timings. During retrieval, two responses to the identical request carried `times.data` of 0.428 and 0.184 and were otherwise the same; only the second was kept, so this is an observation, not something the repository can show. We record both the raw hash and a hash of the data alone.
2. **A point is a grid cell.** The third coordinate in the response is the cell's mean elevation. For Pavlodar it is 111 m (plausible). For Almaty it is 2478 m, although the city lies near 800 m: the cell averages mountains and plain, and its temperatures are not the city's.

**Explanation (an inference; Gemini did not confirm its method).** The numbers fit the Theil–Sen slope and a Mann–Kendall test applied to the 24 annual values that the API supplies under the `YYYY13` keys, with the variance of the test statistic computed as if there were no ties. Those API values are rounded to two decimals, so ties do occur; correcting for them (variant B, the textbook form) changes the precipitation p-value from 0.275 to 0.274. Annual means computed from the months (variant C) give 0.413, 0.264 and 0.035. The differences are in the second or third decimal and change no conclusion.

**Verdict: REPRODUCED** (slopes, p-values and the FDR value), with the method inferred by enumeration.

### What a more careful test says

Our primary analysis uses annual means computed from the monthly values, the Theil–Sen slope with its 95% interval, and the larger p of two autocorrelation-aware tests (Hamed–Rao modified Mann–Kendall; least squares with AR(1) effective sample size). In this record the annual series show almost no autocorrelation, so the Hamed–Rao p equals the classic p for all three variables, and for humidity the primary p is simply the classic Mann–Kendall p.

| Variable | Slope (95% CI) | Classic MK p | Hamed–Rao p | LSQ AR(1) p | Primary p | q | Detectable at 80% power |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| T2M | -0.0212 (-0.0666 to +0.0464) °C/yr | 0.413 | 0.413 | 0.578 | 0.578 | 0.578 | ≥ 0.095 °C/yr |
| PRECTOTCORR | +0.0048 (-0.0041 to +0.0152) mm/day/yr | 0.264 | 0.264 | 0.147 | 0.264 | 0.397 | ≥ 0.014 mm/day/yr |
| RH2M | +0.1645 (+0.0072 to +0.2934) pp/yr | 0.035 | 0.035 | 0.010 | 0.035 | 0.105 | ≥ 0.190 pp/yr |

**Temperature.** The statement “the trend is not significant” is reproduced. The statement “there is no warming” would not be: the 95% interval runs from -0.67 to +0.46 °C per decade, and a record this short and this variable could only be expected to reveal trends above about 0.95 °C per decade. Warming or cooling of several tenths of a degree per decade is fully compatible with these data.

**Relative humidity.** The only nominally significant trend (p = 0.035) is fragile:

- after correcting for three tests, q = 0.105 (Gemini reported the same);
- leaving out any one of the years 2002, 2008, 2017, 2018, 2024 moves p above 0.05 (to between 0.051 and 0.102; over all 24 single-year removals p ranges from 0.015 to 0.102); 2024 is the most humid year of the record;
- in 1981–2000 the slope has the opposite sign (-0.192 pp/yr), and over 1981–2024 it is +0.027 pp/yr with p = 0.436.

So the humidity increase is a feature of the 2001–2024 window, not an established long-term trend.

q-values: Benjamini–Hochberg across the 13 lags, on autocorrelation-adjusted p-values.

- **Lag 0 and lag +1: REPRODUCED** (−0.316 and −0.185). Gemini's “lag +1” is temperature leading humidity; the opposite direction is much weaker (k = −1: -0.081).
- **“About −0.04 at 2+ months”: PARTLY.** It holds at k = +2 (-0.045) but not as a rule: k = +3 gives -0.090, k = +6 gives +0.036.
- **Multiple testing.** Thirteen lags were scanned. After Benjamini–Hochberg correction with autocorrelation-adjusted p-values, the lags that remain significant for T–RH are: +0, +1.
- **The pattern does not generalise to other pairs.** For precipitation paired with later humidity: k = +1: +0.33, k = +2: +0.16, k = +3: +0.13, k = +4: +0.19; significant after correction at k = +1, +2, +4 (not at +3). So “nothing beyond one month” describes the temperature–humidity pair only. This is not offered as evidence for atmospheric memory either: see below.

**What may be said:** in this grid cell, temperature anomalies and the following month's humidity anomalies are weakly negatively correlated. **What may not:** that temperature drives next month's humidity, or that the atmosphere has (or lacks) long-term memory. A lag is a timing statistic; monthly means cannot resolve sub-monthly processes; persistence of either series alone spreads a same-month association onto neighbouring lags.
